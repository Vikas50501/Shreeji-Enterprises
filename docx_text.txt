SECTION 2  ·  THE CHALLENGE
A conventional rod protects the structure. What protects the equipment?
A lightning strike delivers an enormous, extremely fast burst of current. A conventional lightning rod attracts that strike and carries the current to earth, which protects the structure from direct fire damage. But the surge and electromagnetic pulse that accompany the strike can still destroy the electrical and electronic equipment connected to it.
Telecom towers, weather radars, wind turbines, chemical plants, ships and data-rich buildings are especially exposed, because their equipment is both valuable and sensitive.
SECTION 3  ·  OUR APPROACH
Electric field compensation: preventing the strike, not just surviving it
Instead of waiting for a strike, a field-compensation protector continuously balances the electric charge in the air around it. By gathering charge from its surroundings and slowly returning a very small current to earth, it prevents the build-up that would otherwise launch an upward leader toward the clouds. The result is a structure that is far less likely to be struck in the first place.
Passive operation
No external power supply needed.
Continuous compensation
Balances the surrounding electric field at all times.
Prevention first
Aims to prevent the strike instead of absorbing its full current.
Gentler on equipment
Designed to reduce destructive thermal, electrodynamic and electromagnetic effects.
Low maintenance
Periodic inspection is all that is typically required.

Coverage depends on the model, mounting height and local regulations. Typical protection radii range from about 25 m to 120 m. As a general rule, devices are mounted several metres above the highest point to be protected. Detailed design is provided for each project.

SECTION 8  ·  FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Q.  How is field-compensation lightning protection different from a conventional lightning rod?
A conventional rod attracts the strike and carries the full current to earth, which can still damage connected electronics. A field-compensation device continuously balances the electric field around it, aiming to prevent the upward leader that leads to a strike. It is passive and needs no power.
Q.  Can any system protect a structure 100%?
No lightning protection system can honestly promise absolute protection. Performance depends on correct design, mounting height, earthing and the surrounding structures. We provide a design review for each project.
Q.  Do I also need surge protection?
Yes. Direct protection should be combined with ground filtering, proper earthing and surge protective devices on power and data lines to handle indirect effects.

SECTION 4  ·  DETECT &amp; LOCATE FAULTS
From early warning to exact location
Insulation Fault Location
Fault locators work with an insulation monitoring device in unearthed systems. They use measuring current transformers to detect the test-current signal and trace it to the faulty feeder. The fault can be found in a short time and while the plant keeps running, avoiding a shutdown just to search for the problem.
Residual Current Monitoring (RCM)
In earthed systems (TN and TT), residual current monitors measure the sum of all conductors except the protective earth through measuring current transformers. They notify the user before the shutdown threshold of the residual current device is reached, so insulation deterioration is spotted early and unexpected trips are avoided.
Neutral Earthing Resistor (NGR) Monitoring
A neutral earthing resistor limits the maximum fault current to avoid damage and danger from earth faults, while still allowing protective relays to operate correctly. NGR monitors measure voltage, current, continuity of the NGR connection and phase-to-ground voltage. An early change in the NGR value lets maintenance be planned and reveals hazards that were previously unknown.

SECTION 8  ·  FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Q.  What is insulation monitoring and why is it needed?
It continuously measures the insulation resistance of a power system and warns you when it falls, so a developing fault can be fixed before it causes a shutdown, a fire or an electric shock.
Q.  What is the difference between insulation monitoring and residual current monitoring?
Insulation monitoring is used on unearthed (IT) systems; residual current monitoring is used on earthed (TN and TT) systems. Both give early warning, but they work differently.
