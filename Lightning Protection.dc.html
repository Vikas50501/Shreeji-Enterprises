A conventional rod protects the structure. What protects the equipment?

A lightning strike delivers an enormous, extremely fast burst of current. A conventional lightning rod attracts that strike and carries the current to earth, which protects the structure from direct fire damage. But the surge and electromagnetic pulse that accompany the strike can still destroy the electrical and electronic equipment connected to it.

Telecom towers, weather radars, wind turbines, chemical plants, ships and data-rich buildings are especially exposed, because their equipment is both valuable and sensitive.

Electric field compensation: preventing the strike, not just surviving it

Instead of waiting for a strike, a field-compensation protector continuously balances the electric charge in the air around it. By gathering charge from its surroundings and slowly returning a very small current to earth, it prevents the build-up that would otherwise launch an upward leader toward the clouds. The result is a structure that is far less likely to be struck in the first place.

Marine Lightning Protection System

A passive system for yachts, ships and offshore vessels. It collects electrostatic charge and routes it away, balancing the electric field around the vessel so that the upward leader that triggers a strike does not form. Vessels are protected against both direct strikes and the electromagnetic pulses that can damage navigation and communication electronics.

  • Protection of mast, hull fittings and onboard electronics
  • Designed for the salt and humidity of the marine environment
  • Used together with a ground filter and surge protective devices for complete coverage

Typical applications: yachts, commercial vessels, offshore platforms, ports

Defend against the effects you cannot see

High-Frequency Ground Surge Filter

A high-reactance filter that blocks high-frequency surges travelling through the earth after a lightning discharge, an electromagnetic pulse or another source. It protects the equipment inside the structure from indirect effects.

Typical applications: telecom sites, control rooms, data centres, vessels, industrial plants

Down-Conductor Surge Connector

A protective element fitted on the down-conductor cable that minimises high-frequency induced surges generated by atmospheric discharge, electromagnetic pulses and other causes.

Typical applications: general structures served by a down-conductor
