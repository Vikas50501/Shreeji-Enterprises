Two types of electrical system. Two ways of monitoring.

Electrical systems are generally either earthed (TN and TT systems) or unearthed (IT systems). Each calls for its own monitoring method. Our range covers both, together with the measuring and communication components that tie them into a single view of system health.

An insulation monitoring device continuously measures the insulation resistance of an unearthed system and raises an alarm if it falls below a set value. It is connected between the system and the protective earth conductor; a small measuring signal is applied and evaluated by the measuring circuit. Deterioration is detected early and reliably, long before it causes a shutdown or hazard.

In earthed systems (TN and TT), residual current monitors measure the sum of all conductors except the protective earth through measuring current transformers. They notify the user before the shutdown threshold of the residual current device is reached, so insulation deterioration is spotted early and unexpected trips are avoided.

From early warning to exact location

Insulation Fault Location

Fault locators work with an insulation monitoring device in unearthed systems, using measuring current transformers to trace the test-current signal to the faulty feeder while the plant keeps running.

Residual Current Monitoring (RCM)

In earthed systems (TN and TT), RCM notifies the user before the shutdown threshold of the residual current device is reached, avoiding unexpected trips.

Neutral Earthing Resistor (NGR) Monitoring

NGR monitors measure voltage, current, continuity and phase-to-ground voltage, letting maintenance be planned before hazards grow.
